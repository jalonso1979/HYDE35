What the VV-2013 archive actually contains
------------------------------------------
* Horsemen_Bairoch_Data.xlsx
    Single sheet "Bairoch et al data" — European city populations
    (thousands) for 800-1850 from Bairoch et al. (1988). Columns are
    centennial population checkpoints. There are NO mortality columns,
    NO manor-level identifiers, and the smallest spatial unit is a
    European city, not an English manor.

* Horsemen_Online_Appendix.pdf
    Theoretical appendix; describes a Malthusian two-sector model
    with simulated birth/death schedules. Mentions "mortality" 15
    times, all in the context of model-simulated death-rate
    schedules. The strings "Hatcher", "Bailey", "Halesowen",
    "Westminster", "Winchester", and "manorial" do NOT appear.

* Horsemen_REStud.pdf (main paper)
    The only manorial reference is a single footnote (#52) citing
    Kelly and Ó Gráda (2008) — VV did NOT collect or distribute
    manor-level mortality themselves. Their empirical pieces use
    Bairoch city populations and aggregate continental mortality
    estimates from secondary literature.

Conclusion
----------
The VV-2013 "Three Horsemen" replication archive does not contain
manorial mortality data. The paper relies on aggregate / city-level
inputs and a simulated mortality schedule. The Hatcher-Bailey
manor-level death-rate series targeted by Task 4 was never
included in the VV-2013 replication deposit, contrary to the
working hypothesis.

The cascade therefore advances to Tier B:
  Task 5  - Campbell (2016) "The Great Transition" data +
            Hatcher (1986) "Mortality in the 15th Century" OCR.
