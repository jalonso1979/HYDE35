What the VV-2013 archive actually contains
------------------------------------------
* Horsemen_Bairoch_Data.xlsx
    Single sheet "Bairoch et al data" — European city populations
    (thousands) for 800-1850 from Bairoch et al. (1988). Columns are
    centennial population checkpoints. There are NO mortality columns,
    NO manor-level identifiers, and the smallest spatial unit is a
    European city, not an English manor.

* Horsemen_Online_Appendix.pdf
    Theoretical appendix; describes a Malthusian two-sector model
    with simulated birth/death schedules. Mentions "mortality" 15
    times, all in the context of model-simulated death-rate
    schedules. The strings "Hatcher", "Bailey", "Halesowen",
    "Westminster", "Winchester", and "manorial" do NOT appear.

* Horsemen_REStud.pdf (main paper)
    The only manorial reference is a single footnote (#52) citing
    Kelly and Ó Gráda (2008) — VV did NOT collect or distribute
    manor-level mortality themselves. Their empirical pieces use
    Bairoch city populations and aggregate continental mortality
    estimates from secondary literature.

What the Hatcher 1986 PDF actually contains
-------------------------------------------
The article is a SINGLE-SITE monastic study, not a manorial
mortality compilation. Coverage:

* Christ Church Priory, Canterbury — Benedictine monks only
* Period: January 1395 – May 1505 (NOT 1348; cannot anchor BD)
* Population at risk: 414 monks tracked over 110 years
* Quoted annual CDR series exists only as Figure 1 (chart,
  not tabulated); requires manual digitisation to extract a
  yearly series.
* Tables 1-5 give seasonality index, cohort life-table death
  rates (1000qx), Princeton model-fit, and Comparative
  Medieval English life-tables — none of these is a
  manor-year × mortality_rate × sample_size table.

The author EXPLICITLY states (p. 38): "we cannot even tell
whether the experience of Christ Church monks was similar to
that of other English monastic houses, for no comparable
figures have been collected."

Crisis years identifiable from body text:
  1457 (CDR 189/1000, severe pestilence)
  1471 (CDR 139/1000, plague)
  1485 (CDR 116/1000, sweating sickness)
  1487, 1501 (plague, levels not quoted)
Average fifteenth-century CDR: 33.12 / 1000.

Conclusion: Hatcher 1986 cannot supply ≥30 manor-years across
≥2 manors, and provides no observations within the 1340-1349
Black Death anchor window.

What the Kelly & Ó Gráda 2014 PDF actually contains
----------------------------------------------------
The paper is a REGRESSION analysis on three underlying micro
datasets (which are NOT redistributed by the authors):

* Page (2003): 12,378 inheritances on 77 Winchester manors,
  1263-1349 — gated at UK Data Service (Study 4086).
* Campbell (2005, Past & Present 188): ~1,800 IPMs of nobles
  without adult heirs, 1300-1349 — embedded in the article;
  no replication file.
* Gottfried (1978, monograph): ~15,000 Norwich diocese wills,
  1430-1480 — book, no machine-readable file.

Crop-yield covariates are from Campbell (2007) at cropyields.ac.uk
(yield data only, registration-gated). The paper itself prints
only regression coefficients (Tables 1-4), aggregate plots
(Figures 2, 3, 5, 6), and a 19-year "mortality after bad harvests"
summary table (Table 5) covering 1549-1800 — no medieval
manor-year mortality series is published.
