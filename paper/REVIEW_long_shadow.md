# Critical Review — *Volcanic Forcing and the Pre-Industrial Malthusian Trap* (Alonso Ortiz & Da-Rocha)

## Verdict

The core empirical contribution is real and, in its load-bearing parts, survives scrutiny. The exogeneity argument for decade-summed Sigl-Toohey VSSI is sound; the crop-dominant-late demographic-margin coefficient (≈ −9.7×10⁻⁵/Tg) reproduces, is not merely proxying contemporaneous mean temperature, survives the Webb wild-cluster bootstrap with a healthy 45–65 country cluster count, and survives the post-1700 restriction (p ≤ 0.018 in every cut). The long-shadow result — pre-industrial inter-annual climate volatility predicting modern population growth, with a sign-flipping modern-window placebo and a non-growing-season decomposition — is genuine and well-constructed (the regressor is pure climatology and the modern outcome is real census data, so HYDE population-as-input endogeneity does not contaminate it). The Boserup section is, if anything, *under*-sold rather than over-sold: the KK10 population-independent signal is small but real and honestly characterized. This is a publishable paper with a novel dataset and a defensible headline.

The single biggest threat to the headline is not an estimation error but a **false robustness claim in the abstract and conclusion**: the paper states that *both* the crop-dominant-late and pastoral/mixed-late coefficients are "robust across every stress test we run" and "surviving restriction to the post-1700 decadal portion of HYDE," while the paper's own Appendix L.3 reports — correctly — that the pastoral coefficient collapses to p = 0.95 post-1700 because its full-panel significance comes entirely from pre-1700 century-resolution cells. A referee who reads the abstract and then the appendix will find them in direct contradiction on a headline number. This is the kind of self-inflicted wound that sinks otherwise-sound papers in review.

Beyond that, the manuscript carries a cluster of internal-consistency defects that are individually fixable but collectively erode trust: a 3% vs 10% Tambora-magnitude contradiction repeated in the conclusion; a stale-coefficient problem where the headline figure (8 controls) and the quoted numbers/tables (7 controls) disagree; a "panel VAR" label for what is contemporaneous single-equation FE-OLS; an R²=0.45 that is the full pathway-FE model fit mislabeled "univariate" in one passage; and two genuine code bugs (a clustering-on-row-index error that inflates two Stage-2 significance claims, and an inverted lead/lag IRF). None of these overturns the central finding, but several change reported p-values and at least two retract specific significance claims. The paper needs a consistency pass before it is referee-ready, not a re-estimation.

## Blockers

**1. The abstract/intro/§3/conclusion claim the pastoral/mixed-late coefficient survives post-1700; the paper's own appendix shows it collapses to p ≈ 0.95.**
*What:* Abstract (long_shadow.tex:29) — "robust across every stress test we run"; intro (:48), §3 (:254), conclusion (:427) — "both surviving restriction to the post-1700 decadal portion of HYDE." But `joint_var_post1700_results.parquet` and Appendix L.3 (:1098) show the pastoral pop coefficient goes from −5.5×10⁻⁵ (p=1.7×10⁻⁶, full panel) to −1.1×10⁻⁶ (p=0.95, 1700–1900), −4.9×10⁻⁶ (p=0.74, 1750–1900), −2.2×10⁻⁵ (p=0.49, 1800–1900) — never significant in any post-1700 window. Appendix line 1098 states this verbatim: "The pastoral/mixed demographic margin collapses to p = 0.95 post-1700: that significance came entirely from the century-resolution cells."
*Why it matters:* This is a headline coefficient asserted as stress-test-robust in the abstract and conclusion and directly refuted by the authors' own robustness table. It is the first thing a careful referee will catch, and it casts doubt on the other "robust across every test" claims.
*Evidence:* Confirmed by reproduction from `analysis/data/joint_landuse_var_panel.parquet` via `joint_var_post1700.py`; only the crop-dominant-late coefficient survives every cut.
*Fix:* Restrict the post-1700-survival and "robust across every stress test" claims to the crop-dominant-late coefficient. State explicitly that the pastoral/mixed-late demographic margin is Bonferroni-significant on the full panel but is identified off pre-1700 century cells and does not survive the post-1700 restriction. Note the paper already has the correct framing in the Discussion at :422 ("the magnitude survives every robustness slice") — propagate that wording, but be careful that "magnitude survives" ≠ "significance survives."

**2. The reproduction pipeline does not regenerate the lead result.**
*What:* `run_all.py` orchestrates a different paper ("The Long Shadow of Seasonality," six seasonality exercises). The joint-VAR / Tambora modules (`joint_landuse_var.py`, `joint_var_bootstrap.py`, `joint_var_climate_pathways.py`, `joint_var_post1700.py`, `structural_3eq.py`) appear in neither `run_all.py`, the Makefile analysis/robustness recipes, nor `REPRODUCE.md`. Separately, `make robustness` (Makefile:113) lists `joint_landuse_var_panel.parquet` as a prerequisite with **no rule to build it** — verified: after `make clean` (which deletes `$(DATA)/*.parquet`), `make -n robustness` aborts with "No rule to make target … needed by 'robustness'." It currently "works" only because the parquet exists as an untracked manual artifact.
*Why it matters:* `make all` (which depends on `robustness`) cannot reproduce the paper's most-cited number from a clean tree; it errors rather than builds. For a package whose Makefile advertises a full pipeline, this is a hard reproducibility failure on the headline result.
*Evidence:* `grep joint_landuse_var/joint_var/structural_3eq` returns 0 hits in `run_all.py` and `REPRODUCE.md`; only Makefile:113 (a dangling prerequisite). The parquet, results, and `figJ` are all git-untracked.
*Fix:* Add an explicit Make rule that runs `joint_landuse_var.py` to build `joint_landuse_var_panel.parquet` and its outputs, chain the four joint-VAR robustness modules plus `structural_3eq.py` in dependency order, repoint or retire `run_all.py` to the actual paper, and document the run order in `REPRODUCE.md`. Commit (or DVC-track) the headline parquets/figures.

## Major issues

**1. Headline coefficient is stale / spec-inconsistent: figure (8 controls) vs text & tables (7 controls).** `joint_landuse_var.py:218–219` uses 8 controls (adds `p_sd_dev`), producing crop = −9.96×10⁻⁵, pastoral = −5.81×10⁻⁵, and this is what feeds the headline Figure `figJ_joint_landuse.pdf` (:258) and the structural calibration (`structural_3eq.py:86`, which reads `joint_landuse_var_results.parquet`). But the prose (−9.7/−5.5, :29/:48/:254/:427), Table tab:joint-var, the bootstrap table (L.2: −9.65/−5.55), and the post-1700 table all reflect the **7-control** spec used by `joint_var_bootstrap.py:47–48` and the other robustness modules. The gap (~3–5%) is not reconcilable as rounding (−9.96 rounds to −10.0, not −9.7). A replicator re-running the primary module recovers −10.0/−5.8 against a text that says −9.7/−5.5; the figure beside the headline number plots a different specification than the number. The displayed estimating equation (:215) also lists only σᵀ, omitting the precipitation-volatility term the headline module actually includes. *(Several lenses raised this; the alarming sub-claim that the bootstrap parquet is "stale" is refuted — that parquet correctly stores the 7-control values. The real defect is the figure/structural-model vs text/table control mismatch.)* **Fix:** pick one canonical control set, use it in *all* joint-VAR modules, regenerate `figJ` and the structural inputs, and make eq:joint-pop list exactly the volatility terms estimated.

**2. "Three-equation panel VAR" is contemporaneous single-equation FE-OLS, not a VAR.** Each equation is fit independently by `sm.OLS` (`joint_landuse_var.py:196–206`, looped per LHS); the only lagged terms are level regressors; climate and the identifying VSSI enter strictly contemporaneously to the growth interval. No companion matrix, no IRF, no SUR, no ordering (grep for companion|cholesky|irf|sur returns zero). The label appears in the abstract, subsection title, table/figure captions, conclusion, and appendix. It invites referee demands (lag-order selection, stability, ordering robustness, system IRFs) the estimation cannot satisfy and obscures that the demographic identification is purely contemporaneous (same-decade eruption vs same-decade growth). The dynamic IRF (:268) is a *different* single-equation script. **Fix:** rename to "a three-equation system of reduced-form within-country panel regressions" (or seemingly-unrelated panel regressions), state climate/VSSI enter contemporaneously with no lag structure, and reserve VAR/IRF language for the distributed-lag exercise. No estimate changes.

**3. Tambora magnitude is internally contradictory (3% vs 10%) and one figure is mislabeled "annualised."** With β ≈ −9.7×10⁻⁵/Tg on annualised growth and decade-summed VSSI, a 30-Tg decade → 2.9% cumulative (matches the abstract :48 and :298). But :263 and the conclusion :427 state "~10% cumulative population loss" for a Tambora-class shock — which would require ~103 Tg statically, or ~5.8% even at the paper's own 60-Tg Tambora definition (:38). The same shock and coefficient cannot give both 3% and 10%, and the larger, unsupported figure sits in the closing summary. Additionally, :263's "10 Tg → roughly 1% less *annualised* population growth" mislabels the cumulative-over-decade figure as annualised (true annualised = 0.097%/yr, ~10× off). *(One verifier notes the 10% is reconcilable via the dynamic IRF peak at h=+2, β̂=−36×10⁻⁵: 30·−36×10⁻⁵·10 ≈ −10.8%; if that is the intended basis, the paper must say so — but see Major #4, the IRF horizon is itself inverted, so this rescue is currently unsafe.)* **Fix:** standardize on one Tambora-class mass (state 30 vs 60 Tg) and the matching cumulative figure (3% at 30 Tg / ~5.8% at 60 Tg); fix :263 and :427; relabel :263's "annualised" as "cumulative over the decade." If invoking the IRF peak for 10%, show the derivation and fix the IRF first.

**4. Dynamic Sigl IRF reverses the lead/lag sign convention; "peaks +2 decades post-eruption, passes parallel trends" is built on future-VSSI coefficients.** `sigl_event_study_dynamic.py:48` uses `shift(-h)`, which places VSSI from period t+h into row t. The horizon-h response requires V_{t−h} = `shift(+h)`. Reproduced: under the correct convention, the significant −3.63×10⁻⁵ (p=2.5×10⁻⁵) coefficient sits at a **lead** (growth depressed two decades *before* the eruption) — a pre-trend / reverse-causality signature that would *fail* parallel trends — while the genuine post-event horizons are near zero. The F=0.33 "parallel trends" test (:268) is run on what are actually the true post-event lags. This inverts the interpretation of a named dynamic cross-check (Fig 15). The contemporaneous headline (h=0) is unaffected. **Fix:** use `shift(+h)` (or relabel the axis), re-run `estimate_irf`, re-test parallel trends on the genuine leads, and revise §sec:volcanic. Do not use the IRF peak to justify the 10% Tambora figure until this is corrected.

**5. Stage-2 Malthus regressions cluster on the row index, not country; two Table 1 significance claims do not survive correct clustering.** `preindustrial_malthus.py:134` and `preindustrial_malthus_extended.py:123` pass `cov_kwds={"groups": d.index.values}` (unique per row) instead of `d["iso3"]`, collapsing the cluster-robust SE to a per-observation estimator. Every other module in the codebase uses `iso3`. Under correct country clustering: crop-dominant density p 0.065 → 0.207 (loses *), high-density T-volatility p 0.035 → 0.062 (loses **), pastoral density p 0.002 → 0.010 (survives), and the pooled extended-sample headline γ_T moves from p<10⁻¹⁹ → p=3.9×10⁻¹⁰ and δ_T from p<10⁻³ → p=0.035. The Table 1 footnote (:197) explicitly claims "standard errors clustered by country," which the code does not do. **Fix:** change to `groups=d["iso3"].values` in both files, re-run, update Table 1 and the subperiod appendix p-values (point estimates unchanged), and re-state the §sec:stage2 narrative for the two sub-results that fall below 5%.

**6. Two-way "spatial" clustering: nesting artifact misread as a substantive tightening, plus wrong df.** Paper :159 sells the subnational result: "the slope p-value falls from 0.019 to 0.0001 because the latitude band picks up climate-correlated noise." But each country sits in exactly one 10° latitude band, so country is nested in band, the intersection clustering equals the country clustering, and the Cameron-Gelbach-Miller formula V₁+V₂−V₁₂ degenerates to the bare 11-cluster lat-band SE (verified to machine precision: V₁₂ = V₁ = 0.028588; two-way SE = V₂ = 0.016257). The shrinkage is mechanical, not noise absorption — and with positive spatial correlation a coarser cluster should *inflate*, not deflate, the SE (the non-nested Malthus regression in the same file shows the expected ratios 1.07–2.06). Separately, `spatial_se_robustness.py:44` sets `df = len(set(c1))−1 = 75` (country count) instead of `min(G1,G2)−1 = 10`, overstating precision ~20×: at t=4.10, df=75 → p=0.0001 (the quoted value), df=10 → p=0.002. The sign and significance survive (p≈0.002 at correct df), so this weakens rather than overturns the subnational claim. **Fix:** state that two-way clustering reduces to lat-band clustering under nesting; drop the "tightens inference / picks up noise" mechanism; report the correct df (p≈0.002); flag the 11-cluster few-clusters concern; for genuine spatial robustness use sub-unit centroids (non-nested two-way) or an actual Conley HAC SE.

**7. R²=0.45 is the full pathway-FE model fit, not the climate variable's predictive content; one passage mislabels it "univariate."** On N=154 the univariate σ_v regression gives R²=0.33; adding the four pathway dummies raises it to 0.45 (pathway FE alone = 0.13); on the full N=196 sample univariate R² is 0.18. The paper's own Table tab:shadow (:617 "Yes" FE row) and caption (:622) correctly attach 0.45 to the pathway-FE model — but :415 reads "The univariate σ_v^T → log pop growth R² = 0.45," which is a flat factual error, and the abstract (:29), intro (:50), conclusion (:429), and figure caption (:377) frame 0.45 as "the single strongest cross-country predictor," attaching the full-model fit to the variable's standalone strength (overstated 1.4×–2.4×). **Fix:** delete "univariate" at :415; report the univariate climate R² (0.18 full / 0.33 N=154) as the predictor's explanatory power and present 0.45 explicitly as climate + pathway-FE.

**8. −9.7×10⁻⁵ is presented as both the joint-VAR and the single-equation value, implying exact cross-check agreement the code does not deliver.** :48/:254 attribute −9.7×10⁻⁵ to the joint VAR; :268 attributes −9.7×10⁻⁵ to the single-equation regression and calls it a "reproduction." But the current joint-VAR parquet is −9.96×10⁻⁵ (crop) and −5.81×10⁻⁵ (pastoral); the single-equation parquet is −9.69×10⁻⁵. The apparent identity (−9.7 = −9.7) is an artifact of quoting the stale 7-control joint number. The two specs the paper offers as independent corroboration are assigned the same number when the code says they differ (−9.96 vs −9.69). **Fix:** report the joint-VAR pathway coefficients at their true values (−9.96/−5.8) distinctly from the single-equation values, and describe the agreement as "closely reproduces," not numeric identity. *(This is the same root cause as Major #1; fixing the control set resolves both.)*

## Minor issues & robustness gaps

- **HYDE interpolation in the demographic outcome (partially confirmed, but material).** For the median crop-dominant-late country, ~55% of post-1700 decade-growth values are a single geometrically-interpolated number (BLR: identical 0.008004 across 1710–1890 while VSSI varies 0–28 Tg). The paper raises this HYDE-input caveat for the Boserup margin (:343) but not for its own dependent variable. Note: the headline does *not* survive only because interpolated decades are inert — dropping them halves the crop-dominant coefficient (−9.65×10⁻⁵ → −5.12×10⁻⁵) though it stays significant (p=0.0012). Disclose the interpolation, report the non-interpolated share, and confirm sign/significance on benchmark-transition decades. The published magnitude is partly inflated by interpolation structure and the effective N is overstated.

- **Pathway heterogeneity vs universal sign (calibrate the framing).** Under orthogonal climate re-clustering all five clusters show significant negative pop-VSSI coefficients (−2.6 to −8.1×10⁻⁵, all p<0.05); the appendix (:1055) concedes the neat one-pathway-per-margin pattern "does not survive." The body and Discussion (:422) handle this correctly (what survives is the demographic-margin response concentrated in dense-agricultural/pastoral systems). The compressed intro (:48, "sharply pathway-heterogeneous … robust to re-clustering") and abstract (:29) could be misread as claiming the full heterogeneity structure reproduces. Echo :422's care. (Magnitude heterogeneity *is* real — 3.1× spread — so "artifact"/"near-universal" would overstate the other direction.)

- **"Institutional channel" causal gloss (:381).** The between-country slope (−2.48) is a cross-sectional partial correlation surviving |lat| + t_mean + pathway FE; it cannot mechanistically isolate institutional persistence from other latitude-orthogonal time-invariant traits. The paper mostly uses predictive language and :410–412 explicitly concedes the cross-section "cannot answer" which mechanism dominates; only the verb "captures the institutional channel" at :381 overreaches. Soften to "is consistent with."

- **Re-clustering basis includes the headline regressor.** `CLIMATE_FEATURES` includes `t_volatility`, constructively identical to the long-shadow σ_v^T. "Orthogonal to HYDE outcomes" is literally accurate (it's a climate primitive), and the demographic gradient is organized by productive-months/storage, not by t_volatility (the strongest responses are in the *lowest*-volatility clusters), so the substantive worry is refuted. Add a one-line footnote noting one of the six features equals the headline regressor.

- **KK10 table N column is mis-sourced (all five rows).** Table tab:kk10-pathways (:356–360) prints HYDE outcome counts (855, 399, 1235, 304, 513) for KK10-outcome rows; correct KK10 N = 675, 315, 975, 240, 405 (HYDE − KK10 = exactly 4 decades per country, matching the "KK10 ends 1850" truncation at :1006). Coefficients/p-values are correct. Fix the N column.

- **Allen "7,398 city-year observations across 10 cities" (:273) overstates the estimation sample ~2.3×.** 7,398 is the raw CSV row count; the estimation panel is ~2,863 city-years across **8** cities (Leiden and Tuscany are in CITY_COORDS but contribute no usable-wage rows). The table caption (:277) already correctly says 8 units. Restate :273 as "~2,900 city-year observations across eight cities, 1421–1850, from a 7,398-row source archive."

- **Welfare coefficient ambiguity.** `welfare_counterfactuals.py:36–38` hardcodes β=−0.285 (country-FE) / γ_T=0.0321 while the +0.95% headline (:298) uses β=−0.325 (city-FE Spec 4); the docstring cites a third pair (−0.29 / 0.046, mixing specs). All are genuine estimates; the inconsistency is which is authoritative. Standardize on the city-FE spec the text reports and fix the docstring.

- **Mortality "country-linear trends" (:303) is actually a single common demeaned trend.** `preventive_positive_check.py:85–86` enters one `trend` column with one slope; country-specific trends would require trend × country interactions. Result is robust either way (m0 0.0235 → 0.0236). Either change the wording to "common linear time trend" or add the interactions.

- **Allen `shift(5)` is a 5-row lag, not a 5-year lag (:209–210).** Only ~10 observations near the Naples (106-yr) and Paris (54-yr) gaps are mis-lagged; the coefficient is unaffected (−0.3247 → −0.3261). Cosmetic; fix by merging on year−5.

- **Conley docstring is false (code-only).** `spatial_se_robustness.py:2` advertises "an approximate Conley spatial SE check"; the code implements only CGM two-way clustering (no kernel, no distance cutoff). The *paper* never claims Conley, so no published claim is unsupported — but rewrite the docstring to describe the method honestly.

- **Table tab:joint-var σᵀ row does not reproduce (~6%).** Paper :242–243 prints +0.00589 (SE 0.00131); current 8-control panel gives +0.00557. The gap spans all three columns (dlns col-2 is ~31% off: +0.00418 vs +0.00288). Same stale-snapshot root cause as Major #1; regenerate the table from the canonical run.

- **External validity not flagged.** The welfare and positive-check mechanism channels are estimated on 6 / 10 European countries; the conclusion (:427) generalizes to "pre-industrial economies" (which in the global VAR include pastoral/tropical pathways the mechanism data never observe). The body scopes honestly, but the limitations section (:417–422) omits this. Add one sentence.

- **"An honest null" section title (:305) — no change needed.** The title refers to the HYDE cropland-share test, which genuinely is a null; the abstract and intro lead with the same emphasis, and the surviving KK10 signal is consistently reported. Optional editorial nicety at most.

## Code & reproducibility

**Works:** The data pipeline is sound and reproducible end-to-end — eVolv2k parsing (Tambora 1815 = 28.08 Tg, Samalas 1257 = 59.42 Tg), decade-summed VSSI alignment (no off-by-one / look-ahead), the fixed 1421–1750 growing-season mask with genuine modern-window placebo recomputation, cropland/cos-lat weighting with correct lon conventions, KK10 exact-year matching, and the panel counts (3,187 subnational, 196 country, 154-country/N=3,385 joint VAR). The Webb wild-cluster bootstrap is correctly implemented (right sign weights, null imposed via restricted residuals re-signed at country level). The Allen city-buffer Malthus coefficient (−0.325, p=0.058), the mortality m0 channel (+2.35%, p=0.009), and the joint-VAR pathway slopes all reproduce.

**Broken:** (a) clustering-on-row-index in both `preindustrial_malthus*.py` (Major #5) — anti-conservative p-values, two Table 1 stars retracted under correct clustering; (b) inverted lead/lag in `sigl_event_study_dynamic.py:48` (Major #4) — the IRF horizon axis is mirror-imaged; (c) `spatial_se_robustness.py:44` wrong df, ~20× precision overstatement (Major #6); (d) the false "Conley" docstring.

**Stale / inconsistent:** The 7-control vs 8-control split between `joint_landuse_var.py` and the three robustness modules means the headline figure and structural calibration use a different specification than the quoted numbers and tables (Major #1). Table tab:joint-var's σᵀ row predates the current panel (~6–31% gaps by column). `welfare_counterfactuals.py` is parameterized off a different Allen spec than the text uses.

**What a replicator hits first:** `make all`/`make robustness` aborts from a clean tree because nothing builds `joint_landuse_var_panel.parquet` (Blocker #2); `run_all.py` and `REPRODUCE.md` orchestrate a different paper and never touch the lead modules; the headline parquets and `figJ` are git-untracked. Re-running the primary module yields −9.96×10⁻⁵, not the text's −9.7×10⁻⁵. These are reproducibility blockers independent of the science.
