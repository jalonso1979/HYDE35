Subject: Long Shadow paper — substantive reframe before submission

Jose-María,

Before we submit I have to flag a substantive change to the paper's central claim that emerged from a pre-submission robustness audit I ran today. I'd like your sign-off before sending it out.

Three exercises on the three-margin cross-pathway asymmetry that anchored §4 in the version you saw:

(a) Re-clustering the pathway typology on climate primitives that are orthogonal to HYDE outcomes (productive months, intra-annual T and P range, mean T, mean P, inter-annual T volatility, all 1421–1750). All five climate-defined clusters now show significant negative VSSI coefficients on log pop growth. The "one-pathway-per-margin" pattern does not survive.

(b) Wild-cluster bootstrap (Webb 6-point, 1999 reps) and a 300-rep permutation test on the twelve pathway×equation slopes. Bootstrap p-values track the asymptotic p-values closely, but under Bonferroni correction (0.05/12 = 0.0042) only two slopes survive — both demographic-margin (crop-dominant late and pastoral/mixed late). The permutation null produces the observed pattern of one significant cropland slope and one significant urban slope 48% and 73% of the time respectively, indistinguishable from random labelings.

(c) Restricting to year ≥ 1700, which drops the 305 century-resolution HYDE cells where the demographic back-projection is partly imputed from cropland. The pastoral demographic margin collapses to p=0.95. The high-density intensive cropland margin weakens to p=0.076. The early-extensifier urban margin to p=0.28. The crop-dominant late demographic margin survives all temporal cuts at p≤0.018.

The three-margin asymmetry as the central finding is not defensible as we had it written.

What I've reframed the paper around — three findings that survive every exercise:

1. Demographic-margin response to volcanic forcing in crop-dominant late and pastoral/mixed late countries. β = -9.7 and -5.5×10⁻⁵ per Tg of stratospheric sulfur injection, both Bonferroni-significant, both robust to pathway typology and HYDE sub-sample. This is the cleanest pre-industrial volcanic-cooling demographic coefficient that has been estimated at country resolution.

2. A Boserupian cropland-expansion finding that fell out of exercise (c) and that I had not seen before. In the post-1700 sub-sample, crop-dominant late countries show a sharply positive cropland-share response to volcanic forcing: β = +3.6×10⁻⁴ per Tg, p=0.031, tightening to +7.8×10⁻⁴ (p=0.003) in 1800–1900. After cold shocks these economies expanded cropland. This is, to my knowledge, the first cross-country pre-industrial test of Boserup's 1965 intensification hypothesis using exogenous climate forcing. It was masked in the full 1500–1900 panel by offsetting pre-1700 century cells, which is why we hadn't seen it.

3. The long-shadow R²=0.45 result — unchanged, robust to latitude and the deep-determinants battery.

§4 is now titled "Volcanic forcing and the Malthusian channel" (was "The cross-pathway asymmetry of climate response"). The joint VAR is retained as the natural multi-equation generalisation of the Malthusian regression that delivers the demographic-margin coefficients, with the off-margin coefficients flagged as failing the robustness exercises. New §4.2.4 "Boserupian cropland response in crop-dominant late economies post-1700" promotes the new finding. New Appendix L documents the three exercises in full.
