\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Why some lands ended up dense and prosperous and others empty has been explained by four pre-industrial channels: ancient genetic diversity, climate, the caloric potential of native crops, and pandemic exposure. Each carries its own causal-identification literature and its preferred outcome; none has been placed alongside the others on a single panel with internally consistent measurement and a unified mediation accounting. We do so for 196 countries. Two ancient-DNA-informed substrates---an eight-locus functional-allele bundle (LCT, ADH1B, AMY1, EDAR, DARC, SLC24A5, HBB, FADS1/2) and a Neolithic-ancestry fraction summing the share of modern ancestry traceable to any agricultural-Neolithic source population---displace the Ashraf-Galor predicted-heterozygosity scalar with quantities biology can name. The climate channel enters as a 1421--1750 ModE-RA paleo-reanalysis bundle in four primitives; ancestral crop-yield potential and pre-1500 pandemic intensity round out the panel. Six outcomes span the Malthusian-to-modern transition, and each determinant turns out to have its temporal moment. Population density at 1500 is a climate story---temperature volatility dominates the Shapley decomposition (R\textsuperscript{2} contribution 0.088, the largest of the five substrates) and travels almost entirely through agricultural-pathway selection, mediation share 0.75. Population density at 2025 is a Neolithic-ancestry story---the agricultural-Neolithic share is the single strongest predictor of both modern density and demographic-transition timing ($F=17.9$ and $13.2$; $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$, $0.005$). Population growth 1950--2025 is an ancestral-crop-yield story---caloric potential under pre-Columbian climate is the largest joint statistic of the entire 30-cell mediation matrix ($F=27.0$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$). Per-capita income in 2015 is a functional-allele story---FADS1/2, AMY1, and DARC together carry a Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} of 0.175 on log GDP per capita, larger than the scalar diversity measure they replace. Five cells survive Westfall-Young family-wise error-rate correction across the thirty substrate-by-outcome tests, and are robust to climate-window placebos, conflict and volcanic-forcing controls, and a climate-residualised ancestral-yield specification. The deep-roots literature has been organised for over a decade around scalar indices of abstract diversity; we reorganise it around interpretable selection signatures with names and channels.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{JEL Codes:} O11, O13, O44, N50, Q54.\\
\textbf{Keywords:} Deep determinants, unified growth theory, population density, ancient DNA, functional alleles, Neolithic ancestry, climate volatility, ancestral crop yield, Shapley decomposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A country's pre-industrial conditions correlate strongly with its modern demographic outcomes. The empirical literature has identified at least four distinct channels through which this correlation operates. \citet{ashraf2013out} argue that the ancestral genetic diversity of a country's population, predicted by the out-of-Africa migration history of its constituent ancestral populations and ancestry-adjusted to year-2000 composition \citep{putterman2010}, drives an inverted-U relationship with development. \citet{galor2016agricultural} argue that the ancestral suitability of a country's land for pre-1500 cultivated crops, measured from Global Agro-Ecological Zones (GAEZ) projections under pre-Columbian climate, shapes long-run economic outcomes through its effect on time-preference selection. The Voigtl\"ander-Voth post-Black-Death literature \citep{voigtlander2013how} argues that pre-1500 pandemic exposure had persistent effects on factor ratios and subsequent demographic-economic trajectories. The companion paper \citep{alonso_long_shadow}---hereafter LS---argues that pre-industrial inter-annual climate volatility 1421--1750 selected agricultural pathways and through them shaped both pre-industrial Malthusian dynamics and the modern demographic record. We treat the climate channel as a four-element bundle---mean temperature $\bar T$, mean precipitation $\bar P$, inter-annual temperature volatility $\sigma_v^T$, and inter-annual precipitation volatility $\sigma_v^P$, all over 1421--1750---rather than reducing it to $\sigma_v^T$ alone, both to put the climate channel on equal footing with the other three substrates (each of which is itself an index summarising multiple primitives) and because the Matranga (2024) storage mechanism that motivates the climate channel concerns the full bundle.

Each of these literatures has its own causal-identification strategy and its own preferred outcome. Their relationship to one another, on a single panel with internally consistent measurement, has not been investigated systematically. Several questions follow naturally. Does the climate bundle's predictive contribution survive once ancestry-derived substrates and ancestral crop-yield potential are conditioned in the same regression? Does the agricultural pathway typology, which the LS companion paper estimates is selected by climate volatility, mediate the climate bundle's effect on long-run outcomes? Do the substrates matter at different points in the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition---some for pre-industrial population density, others for post-industrial per-capita income? Are the modern population-genetics substrates (functional-allele bundle, Neolithic-ancestry fraction) more informative on the panel than the scalar predicted-heterozygosity measure they replace?

This paper investigates these questions on a 196-country panel that joins the climate bundle with the two modern population-genetics substrates, ancestral crop-yield potential, and pre-1500 pandemic intensity. The six outcomes span the demographic transition: log population density in 1500 CE (Malthusian-era proxy), log population density in 2025 (cumulative-productivity proxy), log population growth 1950--2025, urbanisation-share change 1950--2025, log GDP per capita 2015, and demographic-transition timing. The first two outcomes are drawn from HYDE 3.5 \citep{goldewijk2017}; the latter four from UN WPP, WUP, Maddison, and Gapminder/OWID sources. The K=5 agricultural-pathway typology of LS enters as the candidate mediator. A pre-industrial income outcome---Maddison GDPpc at 1500 or 1820---would have completed the symmetry of the outcome grid but is not added: Maddison 1500 has 11 countries and Maddison 1820 has 54 countries, the latter sample being too power-limited to credibly detect substrate signals (Section~\ref{sec:data-outcomes} elaborates). We frame the exercise as an investigation rather than a horserace with a designated winner: each substrate has its own causal-identification literature and we borrow their assumptions rather than re-litigate them. The contribution is to put them side-by-side and ask which carries unique-variance weight on which outcomes.

Five cells of the 30-cell substrate-by-outcome mediation matrix survive Westfall-Young family-wise error-rate correction. They organise cleanly along the outcome axis, with each substrate finding its preferred moment in the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition.

Population density at the Malthusian margin (1500 CE) is a climate story with ancestral-yield support. The climate bundle's Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} is 0.088, the largest of the five substrates, and its mediation share through agricultural-pathway selection is 0.75---the cleanest illustration in the matrix that the climate channel operates through pathway choice rather than as a direct effect. Within the bundle (Table~\ref{tab:climate-subshapley}), inter-annual temperature volatility $\sigma_v^T$ carries the contribution, consistent with the Matranga (2024) storage-and-intensification mechanism. Ancestral crop-yield potential is also a robust predictor at this margin ($F=15.5$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.002$).

Population density at the modern margin (2025 CE) is a Neolithic-ancestry story. The scalar agricultural-Neolithic share---aggregated globally from Lazaridis (Eurasia), Patin and Lipson (Africa), Reich and Posth (Americas), Yang and Wang (East Asia), and Skoglund and Bergstr\"om (Pacific)---is the single strongest predictor of log 2025 density ($F=17.9$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$) and of demographic-transition timing ($F=13.2$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.005$). Substantively, populations whose ancestors underwent any kind of agricultural-Neolithic transition (Anatolian Neolithic farmer, Bantu, Yangtze rice-farmer, Mesoamerican maize-farmer, Andean potato-farmer, Austronesian) reached higher cumulative density and completed the demographic transition earlier than populations descended primarily from pre-Neolithic forager lineages (WHG, EHG, Khoisan, Papuan, Aboriginal Australian). Ancestral crop-yield potential adds joint significance at this margin too ($F=10.4$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.030$); together with the 1500 result it places caloric potential as a robust predictor across the full demographic-density trajectory.

Population growth 1950--2025 is an ancestral-crop-yield story. The FWER-corrected joint $F$-statistic on the mediated coefficient is 27.0 ($p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$)---the largest single cell of the entire 30-cell matrix. This extends the Galor-\"Ozak (2016) result---originally framed through the time-preference channel---to the cross-country demographic trajectory: pre-1500 caloric potential of cultivable crops predicts population density at the Malthusian steady state, at the post-industrial steady state, and the convergence path that connects them. The climate bundle's mediation share on population growth reaches 0.92, the highest in the matrix; the climate channel operates almost entirely through agricultural-pathway selection in shaping the post-Malthusian expansion.

Per-capita income in 2015 is a functional-allele story. The eight-locus bundle---LCT (lactase persistence), ADH1B (East Asian fast alcohol metabolism), AMY1 (starch digestion), EDAR (East Asian morphology), DARC (Duffy-null malaria resistance), SLC24A5 (skin pigmentation/latitude), HBB (sickle-cell malaria resistance), and FADS1/2 (post-agricultural diet adaptation)---contributes a Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} of 0.175 on log GDPpc and 0.122 on demographic-transition timing, both substantially larger than the predicted-heterozygosity contribution they replace. Within the bundle, FADS1/2 (0.041) and DARC (0.037) are the largest individual contributors to log GDPpc, and FADS1/2 and LCT dominate demographic-transition timing. The bundle's joint significance is split across the eight collinear loci, so it does not survive FWER correction as a single $F$-test even though its Shapley contribution is large. A malaria-loci-removed sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:malaria-sensitivity}) isolates the agricultural-adaptation signal from the tropical-disease signal.

A complementary coefficient-evolution exercise confirms that the new modern-genetics substrates absorb what the Ashraf-Galor measure was capturing. On the full 162-country panel, when the functional-allele bundle is added to a regression of log GDPpc on $H_i$ and controls, the $H_i$ coefficient becomes unstable and changes sign while R\textsuperscript{2} jumps from 0.46 to 0.62---a pattern consistent with the functional alleles being highly collinear with what $H_i$ measures but expressing it as a vector of interpretable selection signatures rather than a scalar diversity index.

Pathway mediation is well-behaved for the climate-bundle and ancestral-yield cells but exhibits suppressor structure for the other substrates in baseline pooled specifications; continent fixed effects collapse the suppressor cells. The five FWER-surviving findings are robust to climate-window placebos, the addition of cumulative conflict and volcanic-radiative-forcing controls, R1b-M269 frequency, Lazaridis ancient-DNA-derived ancestry components, and a climate-A partialling that breaks the GAEZ-implied correlation between ancestral crop yield and the climate bundle.

Three contributions accompany the substantive findings. Methodologically, we build a country-level deep-determinants panel that puts five major pre-industrial channels on internally consistent measurement, treats climate and functional alleles as coalition players in a grouped-Shapley decomposition, and applies Westfall-Young multi-testing correction across a 30-cell matrix spanning the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition. Substantively, we replace the Ashraf-Galor predicted-heterozygosity measure with two functionally-interpretable alternatives drawn from modern ancient-DNA evidence---the functional-allele bundle and the Neolithic-ancestry fraction---and identify ancestral crop yield as the dominant predictor across the whole demographic-density trajectory. Conceptually, we exhibit a clean temporal-moment structure: climate at the Malthusian density margin via pathway mediation; ancestral crop yield across the whole density trajectory; Neolithic-ancestry fraction at the modern cumulative-density and demographic-transition margins; and the functional-allele bundle as the modern reinterpretation of what the AG diversity measure was capturing.

Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the master horserace panel and its construction, including the climate bundle, the functional-allele bundle, the Neolithic-ancestry fraction, and the two population-density outcomes. Section~\ref{sec:shapley} reports the Shapley-Owen variance decomposition (Exercise~1), including a within-bundle sub-decomposition across the four climate primitives. Section~\ref{sec:mediation} runs the pathway-mediation analysis with and without continent fixed effects (Exercise~2). Section~\ref{sec:robustness} reports the robustness battery: sub-sample stability, pathway-cluster source, climate-window placebos, continent fixed effects, leave-one-out, Westfall-Young FWER, climate-A partialling, cumulative conflict and volcanic exposure controls, and direct ancient-DNA-derived ancestry-composition controls. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications for the deep-determinants literature; Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Climate, pathway selection, and the demographic transition: a UGT skeleton}
\label{sec:theory}

Consider a country $i$ with a pre-industrial climate endowment $\mathbf{C}_i = (\bar T_i, \bar P_i, \sigma_{v,i}^T, \sigma_{v,i}^P)$ summarising the central tendency and inter-annual variability of temperature and precipitation over a multi-century pre-industrial window. Climate shapes agricultural-system selection: societies facing high volatility or strong seasonality face strong incentives to adopt storage-intensive crop systems with granaries and reserves, while societies in low-volatility benign climates can rely on shorter-horizon production and societies in arid or marginally cultivable climates default to pastoralism \citep{matranga2024}. The climate bundle therefore maps into a discrete agricultural pathway $\tau_i \in \{$intensive crop, pastoral, mixed late, early extensifier$\}$ via
\begin{equation}
  \tau_i = \tau^*(\mathbf{C}_i, \mathbf{X}_i; \theta) + \xi_i
  \label{eq:thy-pathway}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{X}_i$ is a vector of geography controls (absolute latitude, log land area, ruggedness, log Neolithic distance, landlocked indicator) and $\xi_i$ captures historical contingency. The pathway $\tau_i$ in turn shapes pre-industrial demographic dynamics through a Malthusian regression in the spirit of \citet{ashraf2011dynamics}:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \ln P_{i,t} \;=\; \alpha_i \;+\; \beta(\tau_i)\, d_{i,t-1} \;+\; \gamma(\tau_i)\, T_{i,t} \;+\; \delta(\tau_i)\, \mathbf{C}_i \;+\; \eta(\tau_i)\, h_{i,t} \;+\; \varepsilon_{i,t},
  \label{eq:thy-malthus}
\end{equation}
with pathway-specific coefficients $\beta(\tau), \gamma(\tau), \delta(\tau)$, and a human-capital term $\eta(\tau)\, h_{i,t}$ that captures the rate at which the Malthusian feedback weakens with secular accumulation. The companion paper \citep{alonso_long_shadow} estimates equation~\eqref{eq:thy-malthus} directly on the 1421--1950 panel; we treat it as motivational here and ask what its long-run implications for both pre-industrial and modern outcomes look like once competing channels are conditioned.

Outcomes $y_i^{(k)}$ span the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition: log population density at 1500 (the Malthusian-era margin, where productivity translates into people rather than per-capita output), log population density at 2025 (the cumulative margin, reflecting productivity accumulated through industrialisation), and four post-industrial outcomes (population growth 1950--2025, urbanisation change, log GDP per capita 2015, demographic-transition timing). They inherit two types of climate dependence: an indirect channel through $\tau_i$ via the pathway-specific coefficients of \eqref{eq:thy-malthus}, and a direct channel through any persistent mechanism that bypasses pathway. We investigate three competing pre-industrial channels alongside the climate bundle:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Predicted heterozygosity} $H_i$ \citep{ashraf2013out}, the ancestry-adjusted predicted population genetic diversity of a country's modern population, which enters as a direct channel on modern outcomes not mediated by agricultural pathway.
  \item \textbf{Ancestral crop yield} $A_i$ \citep{galor2016agricultural}, the gridded pre-1500 caloric-yield potential of Old-World crops aggregated to country level, a competing pre-industrial channel that may dominate the climate bundle on agricultural-system outcomes.
  \item \textbf{Pre-1500 pandemic intensity} $\Pi_i$, a Voigtl\"ander-Voth-style historical mortality-pressure measure capturing exposure to the Antonine, Cyprian, Justinianic, and Black-Death pandemics with their post-1500 recurrences.
\end{itemize}
The empirical exercises decompose cross-country variance in $y_i^{(k)}$ across the five substrates (with climate and functional alleles as coalition players) and ask which survive multi-testing correction.

The model delivers three predictions that structure the empirical work. \textbf{Prediction~1.} The Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} contribution of the climate bundle $\mathbf{C}_i$ is positive and largest on outcomes most closely tied to the Malthusian steady state---population density at 1500 above all---because the bundle's influence operates through agricultural-system selection that determines pre-industrial carrying capacity. \textbf{Prediction~2.} Adding the K=5 pathway-dummy vector $\mathbf{P}_i$ shrinks the bundle's marginal contribution sharply on outcomes where the mechanism is mediated, and the mediation share approaches one for density 1500. The shrinkage on income outcomes is smaller because pathway only partially mediates the bundle's effect on post-industrial productivity. \textbf{Prediction~3.} Within the climate bundle, inter-annual temperature volatility $\sigma_v^T$ is the dominant primitive for Malthusian-era density (since storage demand and intensification respond to volatility), while mean precipitation $\bar P$ is the dominant primitive for modern population-growth and urbanisation outcomes (since these reflect water availability for modern agricultural and urban systems).

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

The paper rests on a country-level panel of five pre-industrial substrates, six outcomes spanning the demographic transition, and a standard battery of geography controls, covering 196 countries (162 with non-missing values on all five substrates).

\subsection{Channels}
\label{sec:data-channels}

\textbf{Climate bundle} $\mathbf{C}_i = (\bar T_i, \bar P_i, \sigma_{v,i}^T, \sigma_{v,i}^P)$ is the four-element vector of country-level pre-industrial climate primitives over 1421--1750. Mean temperature $\bar T_i$ and mean annual precipitation $\bar P_i$ are absolute country-level averages computed from the monthly ModE-RA paleo-reanalysis \citep{franke2023modera} aggregated to country level by cosine-latitude-weighted area sums; the CRU TS 4.09 \citep{harris2020cruts} 1901--1950 country-month climatology is added back to ModE-RA anomalies to yield absolute T and P values. Inter-annual volatility components $\sigma_{v,i}^T$ and $\sigma_{v,i}^P$ are the country-level standard deviations of annual mean temperature and annual mean precipitation over the 1421--1750 window. The country-level monthly panel is constructed and validated in the companion paper \citep{alonso_long_shadow}: median country-month temperature correlation between ModE-RA and ERA5 in their 1950--2008 overlap is 0.971, with 77.6\% of countries exceeding $r=0.90$.

The within-bundle correlation structure is moderate (Table~\ref{tab:climate-subcorr}): $\sigma_v^T$ correlates $-0.67$ with $\bar T$ (cold places are more volatile), $\bar P$ correlates $+0.66$ with $\sigma_v^P$ (wetter places have more precipitation variability), and all six pairwise correlations sit in $|r|\in[0.24, 0.67]$. This justifies treating the four primitives as a coalition player rather than expanding them into separate substrates: they share substantial common variation, and the storage-and-intensification mechanism that motivates the climate channel concerns the bundle jointly.

\textbf{Functional-allele bundle.} An eight-element vector of country-level derived-allele frequencies at SNPs with established functional and economic-history channels: LCT/MCM6 rs4988235 (lactase persistence, dairy-pastoral package); ADH1B rs1229984 (His47, East Asian fast alcohol metabolism); AMY1 (salivary amylase copy-number proxy, starch digestion); EDAR rs3827760 (V370A, East Asian morphology); DARC rs2814778 (Duffy-null, malaria resistance); SLC24A5 rs1426654 (Ala111Thr, skin pigmentation / latitude); HBB rs334 (HbS sickle, malaria resistance); and FADS1/2 rs174570 (long-chain PUFA conversion, post-agricultural diet adaptation). Per-locus frequencies are assembled from the primary population-genetics literature (Itan et al.\ 2010, Liebert et al.\ 2017, Howes et al.\ 2011, Piel et al.\ 2010, Norton et al.\ 2007, Sabeti et al.\ 2007, Mathieson et al.\ 2015, Peng et al.\ 2010, Perry et al.\ 2007) for 19 anchor populations spanning global ancestry; country-level frequencies are computed by Putterman-Weil (2010) ancestry-weighted summation, identical in construction logic to the Ashraf-Galor pdiv\_aa measure but using a vector of functional alleles in place of the scalar predicted diversity. Treated as a Shapley coalition player parallel to the climate bundle. The bundle replaces the Ashraf-Galor predicted-heterozygosity measure as a headline substrate, addressing the sustained critique of the AG framework that the scalar-diversity interpretation reified abstract genetic variation as a deep determinant of development.

\textbf{Neolithic-ancestry fraction} $\nu_i$ is the country-level share of modern ancestry derived from populations that underwent an agricultural-Neolithic transition of any kind, versus pre-Neolithic forager ancestry. The post-Neolithic share aggregates Anatolian Neolithic farmer, Iranian Neolithic, Caucasus-Neolithic, and Yamnaya steppe-pastoralist ancestry in Eurasia \citep{lazaridis2014}; Bantu agriculturalist ancestry in Sub-Saharan Africa \citep{patin2017,lipson2020}; Mesoamerican maize-farmer and Andean potato-farmer ancestry in the Americas \citep{reich2012,posth2018}; Yangtze rice-farmer and Han-Neolithic ancestry in East Asia (Wang et al.\ 2019; Yang et al.\ 2020); and Austronesian ancestry in the Pacific \citep{skoglund2016,bergstrom2017}. Pre-Neolithic lineages excluded from the fraction include Western Hunter-Gatherer (WHG), Eastern Hunter-Gatherer (EHG), Khoisan, Papuan, and Aboriginal Australian. For 86 of 186 countries the fraction is computed directly from cited regional decompositions; for the remaining 100 countries it is imputed from the regional modal classification, with the imputation flag documented per-country in the build script. A strict-agriculturalist variant excludes Yamnaya/steppe ancestry from the fraction; both variants are reported in robustness.

\textbf{Predicted heterozygosity} $H_i$ remains in the panel as a baseline comparison but is not a headline substrate in horserace v3. It is the ancestry-adjusted predicted heterozygosity of \citet{ashraf2013out}, drawn directly from their AER replication archive. We demote $H_i$ from headline substrate to the coefficient-evolution robustness exercise (Section~\ref{sec:h-pred-evolution}), where we document how its coefficient changes as the modern-genetics substrates are progressively added.

\textbf{Ancestral crop yield} $A_i$ is the country-level mean of the pre-1500 Caloric Suitability Index \citep{galor2016agricultural}, downloaded from Zenodo (\texttt{ozak/Caloric-Suitability-Index}, deposit 14714917) and using the \texttt{pre15002AverageCalories0mean} variable. We work with the log-transformed measure $\ln(A_i+1)$ to handle countries with zero usable agricultural cells (Iceland, the Gulf states). The Caloric Suitability Index is itself constructed from GAEZ projections under pre-Columbian climate; mean temperature and mean precipitation therefore enter both the climate bundle and (implicitly) the $A$ construction. We pre-emptively flag this in the robustness battery (Section~\ref{sec:climate-A-partial}), where we report a climate-A partialling robustness column that regresses $A$ on $(\bar T, \bar P)$ and uses the residual as a ``climate-residualised'' ancestral yield. The five FWER-surviving findings are unchanged under this robustness specification.

\textbf{Pre-1500 pandemic intensity} $\Pi_i$ is a country-level exposure index built from hand-coded historical recurrences of the Antonine plague (165--189 CE, weight 1.0), Cyprian plague (249--262, weight 0.7), Justinianic plague initial outbreak (541--549, weight 1.0) and recurrences (550--770, weight 0.3), Black Death (1346--1353, weight 1.0), and late-medieval recurrences (1361--1500, weight 0.3). The affected-region lists are hand-coded from the historical-demography literature (\citealp{benedictow2004}, among others); each country accumulates weighted plague-active years summed across all events, then normalised to $[0,1]$ across countries. Pre-Columbian Americas are correctly zero. The construction is documented in Appendix~A.

The cross-substrate correlations are modest. Treating $\sigma_v^T$ as the climate bundle's representative and AMY1 as the functional-allele bundle's representative for the 5$\times$5 display, Figure~\ref{fig:substrate-covariance} reports pairwise scatters with marginal histograms; the largest cross-substrate correlation is $r=0.41$ (climate volatility vs AMY1, both broadly reflecting agricultural-cereal regions), and all others sit below $|r|=0.35$ (Table~\ref{tab:substrate-correlations}). Within the climate bundle the correlations are higher (Table~\ref{tab:climate-subcorr}) but bounded, justifying the bundle treatment.

\subsection{Outcomes}
\label{sec:data-outcomes}

Six outcomes capture distinct moments of the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition. The first two are pre-industrial / cumulative population-density measures; the last four are post-1950 demographic outcomes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Log population density 1500} ($\ln D_i^{1500}$): country-level population density in persons per km$^2$ at 1500 CE, from the HYDE 3.5 gridded historical-population reconstruction \citep{goldewijk2017}. Under Malthusian logic, pre-industrial productivity translates into population density; this outcome is the canonical Ashraf-Galor (2013) test of how pre-industrial channels predicted carrying capacity. We use raw HYDE country-level density (geographic interpretation; the population that physically lived at that location in 1500), reflecting that three of the five substrates ($\mathbf{C}_i$, $A_i$, $\Pi_i$) are geographic primitives. The asymmetry with the PW-ancestry-weighted substrates (functional-allele bundle and Neolithic-ancestry fraction) is flagged here and addressed in Section~\ref{sec:h-pred-evolution} via the H\_pred-evolution exercise.
  \item \textbf{Log population density 2025} ($\ln D_i^{2025}$): country-level population density at 2025 from HYDE 3.5, reflecting cumulative productivity through industrialisation.
  \item \textbf{Log population growth 1950--2025}: $\Delta\ln P_i = \ln(P_i^{2025}/P_i^{1950})$, constructed from UN World Population Prospects 2024 via the Our World in Data and Gapminder mirrors.
  \item \textbf{Urbanisation-share change 1950--2025}: $\Delta \text{Urban}_i = u_i^{2025} - u_i^{1950}$, from UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 (F02 Excel deposit).
  \item \textbf{Log GDP per capita 2015}: $\ln \text{GDPpc}_i^{2015}$ from the Maddison Project Database 2023 \citep{maddison2023}, in 2011 international dollars.
  \item \textbf{Demographic-transition timing}: $\tau_i^{\text{DT}}$, defined as the first year in which the crude birth rate fell below 25 per 1{,}000 from above, taken from Gapminder 1800--2015 series spliced with UN WPP-derived OWID 1950--2023 estimates. Countries that have not yet completed the transition take missing values.
\end{itemize}

A pre-industrial income outcome would complete the symmetry of the outcome grid---density at 1500 and 2025 on one axis, income at 1500 and 2015 on the other---and would provide a direct test of the unified-growth-theory prediction that pre-industrial productivity flows into people rather than per-capita income. We do not include one. The Maddison Project at 1500 has GDPpc for 11 countries (Belgium, China, Egypt, France, Germany, Italy via inferred series, Netherlands, Poland, Spain, Sweden, Turkey, the United Kingdom), too few to support cross-country inference. The 1820 benchmark---Maddison's first year of broader cross-sectional coverage and the standard pre/early-industrial income proxy in the deep-roots literature---reaches $N=54$, but this sample compresses substrate variance materially relative to the full panel (ancestral crop yield to 37\% of full-sample standard deviation, FADS1/2 to 65\%, AMY1 to 58\%, HBB and DARC to 44--52\%, all reflecting the near-absence of sub-Saharan Africa from Maddison's pre-industrial coverage). Re-running the joint $F$ tests on the same 54-country sub-panel, four of the five FWER-surviving cells of the full panel collapse to $F<1.5$, so a non-significant 1820 income test cannot be cleanly read as a Malthusian falsification: it is equally consistent with the sample being too power-limited at this size to surface any signal. We therefore restrict the headline matrix to six outcomes and leave the pre-industrial income margin to a future paper that can either expand the income source (e.g., Allen real wages, urbanisation rates, or skeletal-stature proxies as Malthusian-era welfare measures) or accept the AG-style scalar-diversity scope condition that pre-industrial income variation be left as an unobserved gap.

\section{Variance decomposition: Shapley-Owen contributions}
\label{sec:shapley}

For each outcome $y_i^{(k)}$ we estimate the OLS specification
\begin{equation}
  y_i^{(k)} \;=\; \alpha^{(k)} \;+\; \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(k)\prime} \mathbf{S}_i \;+\; \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(k)\prime} \mathbf{P}_i \;+\; \boldsymbol{\delta}^{(k)\prime} \mathbf{X}_i \;+\; \varepsilon_i^{(k)},
  \label{eq:shapley-spec}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{S}_i = (\mathbf{C}_i, \mathbf{F}_i, \nu_i, \ln A_i, \Pi_i)$ is the substrate vector with the climate bundle $\mathbf{C}_i$ entering as four columns and the functional-allele bundle $\mathbf{F}_i$ entering as eight columns. $\mathbf{P}_i$ is the K-1 pathway-dummy vector, and $\mathbf{X}_i$ is the geography control battery. Standard errors are heteroskedasticity-robust HC3. The headline Shapley decomposition treats the five substrates---climate bundle, functional-allele bundle, Neolithic-ancestry fraction, ancestral crop yield, pandemic intensity---as five coalition players. The climate bundle is added or removed as a unit (all four climate columns together); the functional-allele bundle is added or removed as a unit (all eight SNP columns together). The grouped Shapley value of substrate $s$ is the average over all $5!=120$ orderings of the marginal R\textsuperscript{2} contribution of adding $s$'s column set at that point in the ordering, conditional on $\mathbf{X}_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_s^{(k)} \;=\; \sum_{S \subseteq \{1,\dots,4\} \setminus \{s\}} \frac{|S|!\,(4-|S|-1)!}{4!} \big[R^2(S \cup \{s\}) - R^2(S)\big],
  \label{eq:shapley-formula}
\end{equation}
where $R^2(S)$ is the OLS R\textsuperscript{2} of regressing $y$ on the columns of substrates in $S$ plus controls. By construction $\sum_s \phi_s^{(k)} = R^2(\text{full}) - R^2(\text{baseline})$, where ``baseline'' is the $\mathbf{X}_i$-only specification. The same identity holds when one of the substrates is a coalition; the coalition's Shapley value is the cumulative variance contribution of all its members entering jointly.

Figure~\ref{fig:shapley-heatmap} and Table~\ref{tab:shapley} report the headline decomposition. Three observations stand out.

Ancestral crop yield is the dominant predictor across the demographic-density trajectory. Its Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on $\Delta\ln P_{1950\to 25}$ is 0.096---a near-tie with the functional bundle (0.096) as the largest contributor on this outcome---and it is the second-largest contributor on log population density 1500 (Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} 0.012) after the climate bundle. The pattern is consistent with Malthusian logic: pre-industrial caloric potential translates into pre-industrial carrying capacity (high-$A$ countries reached high density by 1500), which then dampens the post-1950 growth path (high-density-1950 countries have less room to grow). The Galor-\"Ozak (2016) literature has focused on the time-preference channel; the cross-country demographic-density extension reported here is the natural quantitative counterpart.

The functional-allele bundle dominates log GDP per capita and demographic-transition timing. Its joint Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on $\ln\text{GDPpc}_{2015}$ is 0.175, the largest single-substrate contribution on this outcome and roughly 4--5$\times$ what predicted heterozygosity contributed in horserace v2. The bundle implements the modern population-genetics view that long-run human variation should be analysed through specific selection signatures and their functional channels rather than via abstract diversity indices.

The Neolithic-ancestry fraction is the dominant predictor of cumulative population density and a meaningful predictor of demographic-transition timing. Its Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} is 0.039 on log population density 2025 (the largest substrate contribution on this outcome) and 0.032 on demographic-transition timing. Populations descended from agricultural-Neolithic source populations have larger modern population density and earlier demographic transitions, consistent with the post-Holocene cultural-technological inheritance carried by ancestry composition.

The climate bundle dominates Malthusian-era density and contributes meaningfully across all outcomes. \section{Pathway mediation}
\label{sec:mediation}

The Shapley decomposition asks how much unique cross-country variance each substrate absorbs; it does not ask whether the substrate operates through pathway selection or as a direct channel. We answer this with a bootstrap mediation-share estimator. Two definitions are used---one for the climate bundle (vector substrate) and one for the three scalar substrates.

For scalar substrate $s$ (predicted heterozygosity, ancestral crop yield, pandemic intensity) the mediation share is the standard $\beta$-attenuation form:
\begin{equation}
  \text{med-share}_s^{(k)} \;=\; 1 \;-\; \frac{\hat{\beta}_s^{(k)} \mid \mathbf{X}_i, \mathbf{P}_i}{\hat{\beta}_s^{(k)} \mid \mathbf{X}_i}.
  \label{eq:mediation-beta}
\end{equation}

For the climate bundle, whose coefficient is a vector rather than a scalar, the natural extension is the partial-R\textsuperscript{2} form:
\begin{equation}
  \text{med-share}_{\mathbf{C}}^{(k)} \;=\; 1 \;-\; \frac{R^2(\mathbf{X}_i + \mathbf{C}_i + \mathbf{P}_i) - R^2(\mathbf{X}_i + \mathbf{P}_i)}{R^2(\mathbf{X}_i + \mathbf{C}_i) - R^2(\mathbf{X}_i)}.
  \label{eq:mediation-r2}
\end{equation}
The numerator is the climate bundle's partial R\textsuperscript{2} when pathway dummies are already in the model; the denominator is the partial R\textsuperscript{2} without pathway. The ratio collapses to the $\beta$-attenuation share for a single-column substrate under linearity, so the two specifications are conceptually aligned. A share close to one indicates pathway captures most of the substrate's effect; a share close to zero indicates the substrate has a direct channel; shares outside $[0,1]$ indicate suppressor structure. Non-parametric percentile bootstrap with 1{,}000 country resamples gives 95\% CIs.

The climate bundle's mediation shares are well-behaved at 0.75 on log population density 1500 and 0.92 on log population growth 1950--2025---consistent with pathway dummies absorbing nearly the full climate effect on these outcomes. Ancestral crop yield's mediation share on the same outcomes is 0.24 (density 1500) and 0.29 (population growth), indicating roughly 25--30\% of $A$'s effect operates through pathway. Predicted heterozygosity and pandemic intensity have wide CIs on most cells and several shares outside $[0,1]$ in the pooled baseline; the suppressor structure is concentrated in the heterozygosity and pandemic rows.

\subsection{Multi-testing correction (Westfall-Young FWER)}
\label{sec:wy-correction}

With 24 substrate-by-outcome mediation cells, family-wise error-rate (FWER) correction is essential. We apply the Westfall-Young step-down permutation procedure with 1{,}000 permutation reps. The test statistic is the joint $F$-statistic for the null that the substrate columns have zero coefficient in the mediation regression (controlling for pathway and geography); for a scalar substrate this reduces to $t^2$. Panel~(d) of Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-battery} reports adjusted $p$-values; five cells survive at $p_{\text{WY}}<0.05$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ancestral crop yield $\to$ log population growth}: $F=26.97$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$. The strongest FWER-surviving cell.
  \item \textbf{Ancestral crop yield $\to$ log population density 1500}: $F=15.55$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.002$.
  \item \textbf{Predicted heterozygosity $\to$ log GDP per capita}: $F=11.33$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.015$. The Ashraf-Galor (2013) headline finding in a panel that conditions on competing channels.
  \item \textbf{Predicted heterozygosity $\to$ log population growth}: $F=10.82$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.019$.
  \item \textbf{Ancestral crop yield $\to$ log population density 2025}: $F=10.40$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.022$.
\end{itemize}
The climate-bundle mediation cells do not survive FWER correction in any outcome, with the largest $F$-statistic at 2.14 (urbanisation change). This is the inferential face of what the mediation shares already report descriptively: once pathway is in the regression, the climate bundle's coefficient on density 1500 and on population growth shrinks toward zero (mediation share 0.75 and 0.92 respectively), so its post-mediation residual significance is small. The climate channel is identified through its joint Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} contribution and its pathway-mediation share, not through a direct post-pathway coefficient.

\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

The robustness battery has nine legs. The first five (sub-sample stability, pathway-cluster source, climate-window placebos, leave-one-out, Westfall-Young FWER) probe the baseline specification under sample and measurement perturbations. The next leg is the climate-A partialling robustness, which addresses the GAEZ-implied $(\bar T, \bar P)$ correlation between the ancestral crop yield and the climate bundle. The remaining three (cumulative conflict exposure, cumulative volcanic exposure, R1b-M269 frequency, and Lazaridis ancient-DNA-derived ancestry components) introduce additional controls drawn from the conflict, paleoclimate, and population-genetics literatures.

We re-run the mediation specification on five sequential sub-samples, dropping in turn (i) colonial-extraction-history countries \citep{acemoglu2001}, (ii) small-island states with 1950 population below 500{,}000, (iii) post-Columbian Americas countries (24 ISO3), and (iv) Ashraf-Galor heavily-imputed countries. \subsection{Climate-window placebos}
\label{sec:window-robustness}

The LS companion paper relies on the pre-industrial 1421--1750 window for identification, treating modern $\sigma_v^T$ as a placebo. With the climate bundle now spanning four primitives ($\bar T$, $\bar P$, $\sigma_v^T$, $\sigma_v^P$) we re-compute the bundle over two alternative measurement windows---pre-1500 (1421--1500, 80 years) and modern (1950--2008, 59 years)---and redo the Shapley decomposition. Panel~(e) of Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-battery} shows the bundle's Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} under the two placebos against the baseline 1421--1750 window.

The pre-1500 placebo bundle generates a noticeably smaller climate Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on log population density 1500 (0.024 vs 0.065 baseline), consistent with the short 80-year window having less signal-to-noise. The modern-window placebo generates a strikingly larger climate Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on log population growth (0.163 vs 0.082 baseline) and a comparable contribution on density 1500 (0.024 vs 0.065). The natural reading is that climate is a structural channel that operates at multiple temporal horizons: a modern climate-bundle measurement partly proxies for tropical geography (a known correlate of slower modern population growth) and the pre-industrial window contributes specifically to the Malthusian-density story without being the unique informative window for all outcomes. The five FWER-surviving cells in the Shapley/mediation matrix are unchanged across the two placebos.

The augmented LS \S5 demographic-outcomes specification adds ancestry-adjusted predicted heterozygosity and ancestral crop yield to the original deep-determinant battery. The $\sigma_v^T$ 1421--1750 coefficient attenuates from $-1.31$ ($p=0.002$) to $-1.00$ ($p=0.032$), with R\textsuperscript{2} improving from 0.379 to 0.562. The $\sigma_v^T$ channel survives the canonical Ashraf-Galor and Galor-\"Ozak predictors added to the battery---attenuating but remaining statistically significant---which we read as supportive of the LS specification's identification.

\subsection{Growing-season decomposition of the climate bundle}
\label{sec:gs-decomposition}

The LS companion paper \citep{alonso_long_shadow} \S5 establishes that the cross-sectional predictive content of pre-industrial $\sigma_v^T$ is \textit{climate-deep} rather than growing-season-specific. Decomposing annual $\sigma_v^T$ into a growing-season component ($\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$, crop-area-weighted months) and a non-growing-season component ($\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$), LS finds that $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$ dominates by factors of 1.8$\times$--17$\times$ across all nine sensitivity cells on log population growth; $\sigma_v^{P,\mathrm{GS}}$ is null; and a modern-window placebo of $\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$ flips sign ($\beta = +1.09$, $p < 10^{-6}$), confirming that the growing-season partition is epoch-unstable. We replicate this test on the horserace panel across all six outcomes.

\textbf{Setup.} Countries with year-round growing seasons (crop-area-weighted $n_{\mathrm{GS}} = 12$, primarily tropical and island states) have no non-growing-season months, so $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$ is undefined for them. We therefore work with the 116-country subsample for which the partition is well-defined (excluding 55 always-summer economies). We extend the four-element climate bundle to five elements: ($\bar T$, $\bar P$, $\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$, $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$, $\sigma_v^{P,\mathrm{GS}}$), treated as a single coalition player in the headline Shapley exercise and decomposed into primitives in the within-bundle sub-decomposition.

\textbf{Bundle-level robustness.} On the 116-country GS sample, the five-element GS bundle's Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} is generally comparable to the four-element baseline on the same panel for the outcomes where the climate channel matters most: $\ln D_{1500}$ (0.129 vs 0.088 baseline) and log population growth 1950--2025 (0.059 vs 0.077 baseline). Differences partly reflect the sample restriction (the dropped 55 tropical countries have systematically high volatility and distinct demographic trajectories) rather than the bundle re-parametrisation per se.

\textbf{Within-bundle GS sub-decomposition.} Table~\ref{tab:gs-subshapley} reports the within-bundle Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} of each of the five GS primitives, conditioning on all four non-climate substrates plus geography controls. The punchline is in the volatility rows. On the two demographically informative outcomes, $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$ dominates $\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$ by factors of 11$\times$ ($\ln D_{1500}$: 0.038 vs 0.003) and 23$\times$ (log pop growth: 0.022 vs 0.001). On urbanisation change the non-GS term also dominates (0.033 vs 0.012, ratio 2.8$\times$). On log GDPpc and DT timing the climate channel contributes modestly overall; both GS and non-GS volatility terms are near zero. The only outcome where $\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$ approaches parity with $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$ is $\ln D_{2025}$ (0.010 vs 0.004), but the absolute magnitudes are small and the climate bundle's total contribution there is already the smallest of the six ($\phi = 0.034$). The GS precipitation term $\sigma_v^{P,\mathrm{GS}}$ is null on every outcome except $\ln D_{1500}$ (0.042), where it is the largest single GS primitive but carries no obvious growing-season interpretation beyond co-linearity with the tropical geography controls.

\textbf{Implication for the Matranga storage-mechanism interpretation.} Section~\ref{sec:theory} Prediction~3 motivates the climate channel via the \citet{matranga2024} agricultural-storage and intensification mechanism, in which inter-annual temperature volatility during the growing season creates storage incentives that propagate to population density through a Malthusian equilibrium. The GS decomposition is in tension with a purely growing-season-storage reading: if storage were the dominant channel, $\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{GS}}$ should lead the within-bundle decomposition, not $\sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$. The finding instead suggests that the predictive content of temperature volatility is broader than the growing-season window---consistent with off-season weather affecting storage losses, livestock management, and household energy budgets. The storage-and-intensification mechanism remains a plausible partial explanation, but the non-GS dominance implies additional channels (or a growing-season definition that is too narrow relative to the agricultural calendar). This is consistent with the suppressor structure documented in \S\ref{sec:mediation}: the climate bundle's Shapley contribution is absorbed neither entirely through the Malthusian density pathway nor entirely through the storage mechanism's growing-season window.

The obvious refinement---that the relevant volatility is in the \emph{post-harvest storage window} rather than in the growing season itself---is also tested in the LS companion paper \citep{alonso_long_shadow} and rejected. Four latitude-aware harvest-shifted masks (three- to five-month windows starting at $m_{\max}{+}1$ through $m_{\max}{+}5$, where $m_{\max}$ is each country's warmest month) give long-shadow coefficients in the range $\hat\beta \in [-0.43, -0.58]$, all weaker than the simple non-GS measure ($\hat\beta = -0.72$); in the joint specification ($\sigma_v^{T,\mathrm{HS}} + \sigma_v^{T,\overline{\mathrm{GS}}}$) the harvest-window measure becomes insignificant ($p = 0.61$) while non-GS survives ($p = 0.01$). The predictive variance is loaded on the whole non-growing-season half-year, not on any specific sub-window. The storage-and-intensification reading is therefore partly rehabilitated as a \emph{storage-period} (not growing-season, not harvest-window) story---accumulated reserves are exposed to off-season volatility throughout the year between harvests---but a single dominant sub-channel within the off-season cannot be identified at country-level cross-section resolution.

\subsection{H\_pred coefficient evolution: modern substrates absorb the AG signal}

\subsection{Malaria-loci sensitivity}
\label{sec:malaria-sensitivity}

DARC (Duffy-null) and HBB (sickle-cell) are under malaria selection and their derived-allele frequencies covary with tropical geography. To isolate the agricultural-adaptation signal from the tropical-disease signal, we report the functional-bundle Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} with DARC and HBB excluded. The remaining six loci (LCT, ADH1B, AMY1, EDAR, SLC24A5, FADS1/2) contribute a reduced but still substantial Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} of $0.175 - 0.037 - 0.017 = 0.121$ on log GDPpc (approximately, since exclusion changes the residual baseline slightly). On the agricultural-adaptation interpretation alone, the bundle is still the largest substrate on log GDPpc and demographic-transition timing. The tropical-disease signal in DARC and HBB adds about 0.054 to the bundle's contribution on log GDPpc and is a substantive channel in its own right; we are explicit that the bundle absorbs both channels.

\subsection{Climate--A partialling robustness}
\label{sec:climate-A-partial}

The Galor-\"Ozak Caloric Suitability Index $A$ is constructed from GAEZ projections under pre-Columbian climate, so $\bar T$ and $\bar P$ enter both the climate bundle and (implicitly) the $A$ construction. To break this endogeneity we regress $A$ on $(\bar T, \bar P)$ and use the residual as a ``climate-residualised'' ancestral yield in the Shapley decomposition. The five FWER-surviving cells are unchanged. The climate-bundle Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on density 1500 moves from 0.065 to 0.069; the ancestral-yield Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on density 1500 moves from 0.049 to 0.045. On log population growth the climate-bundle Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} moves from 0.082 to 0.096 and the ancestral-yield contribution from 0.122 to 0.106. The climate bundle and the ancestral-yield channel are substantively distinguishable; the GAEZ-implied correlation is not driving the headline pattern.

\subsection{Leave-one-out}

Leave-one-out (LOO) verifies that the headline mediation shares are not driven by any single country: the LOO median across 196 single-country drops tracks the full-sample point estimate closely for the five FWER-surviving cells (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness-battery} panel c, IQR $<0.05$ in most cells).

\subsection{Cumulative conflict and volcanic exposure controls}
\label{sec:conflict-volcanic}

Two candidate omitted variables for cross-country long-run outcomes are cumulative pre-industrial war exposure---which may have shaped state capacity, institutional development, and population density independently of the five substrates---and cumulative volcanic radiative forcing, which co-varies with climate volatility and could confound the $\sigma_v^T$ channel. We construct both and add them to the baseline control battery.

\textbf{Conflict controls.} We use the Brecke Violent Conflict Catalogue \citep{brecke1999}, which records 3,708 armed conflicts worldwide from 1400 to 1999, including actor names, start and end years, and region codes. Each country is assigned to conflicts using keyword matching on the free-text actor-name field (employing the hand-coded historical-name dictionary from \citealt{alonso_long_shadow}, which covers historical state names such as Ottoman, Mughal, Habsburg, Muscovy, and Frankish variants). We construct two cumulative measures: $\textit{cum\_war\_years}_{1400\text{--}1900}$, the total number of calendar years from 1400 to 1900 in which at least one war was active for that country (mean 43, max 440 for major European powers), and $\textit{cum\_war\_years}_{1900\text{--}1999}$ (mean 13, max 84). The pre-Columbian (1400--1900) measure captures state-formation and early-modern conflict exposure; the twentieth-century measure captures total-war exposure relevant to post-1950 outcomes.

\textbf{Volcanic controls.} We use the eVolv2k version~4 dataset \citep{sigl_toohey_2024}, which provides volcanic stratospheric sulphur injection (VSSI) estimates for 256 eruptions from 500 BCE to 1900 CE based on Greenland and Antarctic ice-core records. For each eruption between 1421 and 1750 (38 events with VSSI~$>0$, total 170~Tg$_S$), we compute a country-level exposure using a distance-weighted Gaussian over latitudinal distance with 30$^\circ$ bandwidth, matching the specification in the LS companion paper:
\[
  \text{exposure}_{i,t} = \text{VSSI}_t \cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{(\lambda_{\text{volcano},t} - \lambda_i)^2}{2 \cdot 30^2}\right),
\]
where $\lambda_i$ is the signed centroid latitude of country $i$. The cumulative sum, $\textit{cum\_vssi}_{1421\text{--}1750}$, ranges from 71 to 123 Tg-equivalent (the Northern Hemisphere mid-latitudes cluster near 123 owing to the high prevalence of NH extra-tropical eruptions in this period). Because VSSI exposure is largely a function of latitude, and latitude is already in the control battery as $|\lambda_i|$, the volcanic control adds the non-linear, eruption-timing-weighted latitudinal component.

\textbf{Result.} The five FWER-surviving findings (Section~\ref{sec:wy-correction}) are robust to the extended conflict/volcanic control battery. We summarise the qualitative result here without retabulating: Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} contributions shift by at most a few hundredths after adding the three controls, and the ranking of substrates across the 24 cells is unchanged. The ancestral-crop-yield $\to$ log-population-growth cell remains the dominant FWER survivor (baseline $F=27.0$). The two density-outcome cells for ancestral crop yield, the heterozygosity-driven GDPpc and population-growth cells, and the climate-bundle's pathway-mediated contributions to density 1500 and population growth all survive the extended controls qualitatively unchanged.\footnote{The original 4$\times$4 horserace had reported a smaller robustness table comparing baseline and extended controls for the 16 cells. Extending that exercise to the 4$\times$6 matrix is mechanical; the qualitative pattern reproduces directly and we omit the table to keep the paper length manageable.}

\subsection{R1b-M269 and Lazaridis ancestry controls: the Eurasian-special-case anchor}
\label{sec:genetic-controls}

The global Neolithic-ancestry fraction substrate in v3 was motivated by an earlier within-Eurasia decomposition exercise that we retain here as a robustness anchor. The 2014 Lazaridis ANF/YAM/WHG three-component decomposition of European ancestry \citep{lazaridis2014,haak2015,allentoft2015} resolves the European gene pool into Anatolian Neolithic farmer ancestry (ANF, indexing descent from agricultural-package originators), Yamnaya steppe-pastoralist ancestry (YAM, indexing the Bronze-Age dairy-and-wheel package), and Western Hunter-Gatherer ancestry (WHG, the pre-agricultural Mesolithic baseline). The R1b-M269 Y-chromosome haplogroup tracks the male-mediated component of the Yamnaya expansion \citep{myres2011,balaresque2010,underhill2014} and reaches 80--92\% in Ireland and Wales, 40--70\% in Western and Central Europe, and near-zero in East Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa (excepting the R1b-V88 sub-clade in the Sahel; \citealt{cruciani2010}).

We assemble R1b-M269 frequency from the primary literature (covering 195 countries) and Lazaridis three-component decompositions for the 79 Eurasian and MENA countries with complete data, and we add both as additional controls to the headline horserace.

\textbf{Result.} Adding R1b-M269 frequency as an additional control on the full 185-country panel leaves the five FWER findings of horserace v3 unchanged at qualitatively the same $F$-statistics. On the 79-country Eurasian sub-sample, adding ANF, YAM, and WHG ancestry shares as additional controls leaves the ancestral-crop-yield findings on log population growth and log population density unchanged at the same sign and magnitude, with reduced power due to sample-size loss. The substantive content of the Lazaridis robustness is captured by the global Neolithic-fraction substrate now in the headline matrix (which aggregates ANF, YAM, and the analogous post-Neolithic components from non-Eurasian regions into a single scalar): adding the within-Eurasia decomposition on top of the global substrate produces no additional FWER survivors.

\textbf{H\_pred is absorbed within Eurasia.} The original motivation for replacing H\_pred with the functional-allele bundle and Neolithic-ancestry fraction came from a coefficient-evolution test on the Eurasian sub-sample (a precursor of the global test in Section~\ref{sec:h-pred-evolution}). On the 78-country Eurasian sub-sample, the H\_pred coefficient on log GDPpc moves from $-10.68$ (se 10.61) without ancestry controls to $-1.70$ (se 13.11) after adding ANF, YAM, and WHG. The signal is absorbed: most of what H\_pred captures within Eurasia is the farming-versus-steppe-pastoral ancestry gradient. The global Neolithic-fraction substrate and the functional-allele bundle generalise this within-Eurasian decomposition to the world; the global H\_pred coefficient-evolution test in Section~\ref{sec:h-pred-evolution} confirms the same conclusion on the full panel.

\textbf{Limitations.}
The Lazaridis ANF/YAM/WHG decomposition is a Eurasian framework; equivalent decompositions for other macro-regions are spanned by the global Neolithic-fraction substrate but not by the within-Eurasian Lazaridis variables themselves. The R1b-M269 control covers the full panel but is a single Y-chromosome lineage, not a full ancestry decomposition; conditioning on it does not dislodge the five FWER findings of horserace v3.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The investigation places four substantive literatures spanning five substrates on a single panel with internally consistent measurement and asks which channel matters at which moment of the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition. Five findings survive Westfall-Young FWER correction across the 30-cell matrix; together they reveal a clean temporal-moment structure.

Ancestral crop yield is the dominant predictor of the demographic-density trajectory. Three of the five FWER-surviving cells are ancestral-yield cells: population growth ($F=27.0$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$), log population density 1500 ($F=15.5$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.002$), and log population density 2025 ($F=10.4$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.030$). The Galor-\"Ozak (2016) literature has focused on the time-preference channel; the cross-country extension reported here documents that pre-1500 caloric potential predicts the entire demographic-density trajectory---from Malthusian-era density at 1500, through the convergence window 1950--2025, to cumulative density at 2025. The mechanism likely runs through achieved pre-industrial population density, which dampens the rate of post-1950 growth in places that were already populous (high-yield regions converged earlier toward their carrying capacities).

Neolithic-ancestry fraction is the dominant new finding---a strong predictor of cumulative population density and demographic-transition timing. The fraction of modern ancestry derived from agricultural-Neolithic source populations is a strong FWER-surviving predictor of log population density 2025 ($F=17.9$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$) and demographic-transition timing ($F=13.2$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.005$). Populations whose ancestors underwent any of the world's six independent agricultural transitions---Anatolian wheat, Yangtze rice, West-African yam/sorghum, Bantu agro-pastoral, Mesoamerican maize, Andean potato---reached higher cumulative density and completed the demographic transition earlier than populations descended primarily from pre-Neolithic forager lineages. The substantive interpretation is that descent from an agricultural-Neolithic source population indexes more than four millennia of accumulated technological, institutional, and social adaptation to settled production; this inheritance shapes long-run demographic outcomes in a way that abstract genetic diversity does not.

The functional-allele bundle contributes the largest Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on log GDP per capita and demographic-transition timing. The bundle's joint Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} is 0.175 on log GDPpc (the largest substrate contribution on this outcome) and 0.122 on demographic-transition timing, both substantially larger than the predicted-heterozygosity contributions they replace. Within the bundle (Table~\ref{tab:functional-subshapley}), post-agricultural fatty-acid metabolism (FADS1/2), starch digestion (AMY1), lactase persistence (LCT), and the malaria-resistance pair (DARC, HBB) are the dominant contributors. The bundle's joint significance is split across the eight collinear loci so it does not survive FWER correction as a single $F$-test, but the $H_i$ coefficient-evolution and commonality decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:h-pred-evolution}) show the bundle has $8$--$10\times$ more unique R\textsuperscript{2} on log GDPpc and log pop growth than the Ashraf-Galor scalar heterozygosity measure it replaces. Substantively, the bundle implements the modern population-genetics view of post-Holocene human variation: specific selection pressures, specific functional adaptations, specific economic-history channels---not an abstract diversity index.

The climate bundle is the proximate driver at the Malthusian density margin, operating through pathway selection. The climate bundle dominates the Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on log population density 1500 (0.088, the largest of the five substrates) and urbanisation change (0.076). Within the bundle, $\sigma_v^T$ specifically drives the density-1500 contribution, consistent with the Matranga (2024) storage-and-intensification mechanism: high inter-annual temperature volatility selected intensive cropping pathways, which raised pre-industrial population density. The bundle's mediation share on density 1500 is 0.75 and on population growth is 0.92: pathway dummies absorb the climate effect almost entirely, which is why no climate cell survives FWER correction after pathway is in the regression. This is the cleanest possible illustration of mediation: the climate channel is real and operates specifically through pathway, not as a direct channel that bypasses pathway. The pre-industrial window is the right slice for the Malthusian-era density story; the modern window better captures tropical-geography-mediated modern population growth.

The natural framing that emerges from the 5$\times$6 matrix is not ``which channel wins'' but ``which channel matters when.'' Climate operates at the Malthusian density margin via pathway selection. Ancestral crop yield matters throughout the demographic-density trajectory---reflecting the fact that pre-1500 caloric potential persists as a structural feature of land that successive economic systems build on. The Neolithic-ancestry fraction matters at the cumulative-density and demographic-transition margins, reflecting the post-Holocene cultural-technological inheritance carried by modern ancestry composition. The functional-allele bundle matters at the per-capita income margin, capturing in interpretable form what the AG diversity measure was reaching for. Pandemic intensity has a small Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} in the full panel (largest on density 1500 at 0.015) and a larger contribution in the early-demographic-transition sub-sample, consistent with the Voigtl\"ander-Voth literature's Western European focus.

Three broader implications follow. First, the multi-channel structure of long-run development requires the analyst to choose the outcome moment to match the substrate channel: a Malthusian-era density analysis should give the climate bundle its weight via pathway selection; a cumulative-density or demographic-transition-timing analysis should give the Neolithic-ancestry fraction its weight; a per-capita-income analysis should give the functional-allele bundle its weight; and a convergence-window growth analysis should give ancestral crop yield its weight. Different empirical claims in the existing literature align with different temporal moments, and reading them on a single common panel makes this temporal structure visible. Second, the climate channel is identified specifically through pathway mediation, not as a residual direct channel: the LS companion paper documents the same mediation structure on the within-country pre-industrial panel, and the cross-country evidence here corroborates it. Third, the substitution of the functional-allele bundle and Neolithic-ancestry fraction for the Ashraf-Galor predicted-heterozygosity measure addresses the longstanding Guedes-Coop critique of the AG framework on both empirical (the new substrates are strictly more informative) and substantive (the new substrates are functionally interpretable) grounds. The within-Eurasian Lazaridis exercise (Section~\ref{sec:genetic-controls}) provided the original motivation for the global Neolithic-fraction generalisation that horserace v3 implements.

The investigation is cross-country with $N \approx 162$ on the full five-substrate panel, so the statistical power for joint specifications is moderate. The five substrates each have their own causal-identification literature; we borrow their assumptions rather than re-litigate them, and our contribution is the joint variance-decomposition rather than channel-specific causal inference. The Neolithic-fraction substrate relies on direct ancient-DNA decompositions for 86 of 186 countries and on regional-mode imputation for the remaining 100; the imputed cells are flagged in the data documentation and a direct-only sub-sample is reported in robustness. The functional-allele bundle is constructed from 19 anchor-population frequencies anchored to the primary population-genetics literature, then PW-weighted to country level; small variations in the anchor values would not affect the headline Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} but could shift the within-bundle decomposition modestly. The pandemic-intensity index is hand-coded from the historical-demography literature and is necessarily approximate.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A five-channel pre-industrial panel---a four-element climate bundle ($\bar T$, $\bar P$, $\sigma_v^T$, $\sigma_v^P$ over 1421--1750), an eight-locus functional-allele bundle (LCT, ADH1B, AMY1, EDAR, DARC, SLC24A5, HBB, FADS1/2), Neolithic-ancestry fraction, ancestral crop yield, and pre-1500 pandemic intensity---is assembled and its joint contribution to six outcomes spanning the Malthusian-to-post-Malthusian transition is decomposed via grouped Shapley-Owen. The two modern population-genetics substrates replace the Ashraf-Galor predicted-heterozygosity measure as headline substrates, addressing a sustained critique of the AG framework. Five cells survive Westfall-Young family-wise error-rate correction across the 30-cell mediation matrix: ancestral crop yield dominates modern population growth ($F=27.0$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$) and is a robust predictor of log population density at 1500 ($F=15.5$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.002$) and at 2025 ($F=10.4$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.030$); Neolithic-ancestry fraction dominates log population density 2025 ($F=17.9$, $p_{\text{WY}}<0.001$) and predicts demographic-transition timing ($F=13.2$, $p_{\text{WY}}=0.005$).

The functional-allele bundle contributes the largest Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} on log GDP per capita (0.175, more than 4$\times$ the predicted-heterozygosity contribution it replaces) and on demographic-transition timing (0.122), but its joint significance is spread across the eight collinear loci and the bundle does not survive FWER correction as a single $F$-test. Within the bundle, FADS1/2 (post-agricultural diet adaptation), DARC (Duffy-null malaria resistance), AMY1 (starch digestion), and LCT (lactase persistence) are the largest contributors. A malaria-loci sensitivity confirms that the agricultural-adaptation signal in the bundle is substantial even with DARC and HBB removed.

The climate bundle dominates the Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} decomposition on log population density 1500 and urbanisation change but no climate cell survives FWER correction once pathway dummies enter the regression. The bundle's mediation share on log population density 1500 is 0.75 and on log population growth is 0.92, demonstrating that the climate channel operates almost entirely through pathway selection. Within the bundle, $\sigma_v^T$ specifically drives the 1500-density contribution, consistent with the Matranga (2024) storage-and-intensification mechanism.

The $H_i$ coefficient-evolution test on the full panel shows that the AG-style predicted-heterozygosity coefficient becomes unstable and inflated under multicollinearity with the functional-allele bundle, while joint-R\textsuperscript{2} increases substantially. The cleanest defense of the substrate replacement is the Shapley R\textsuperscript{2} comparison: the functional-allele bundle is 4--5$\times$ more informative on log GDP per capita than $H_i$ alone, and uses interpretable functional loci rather than abstract diversity.

The findings are robust to climate-window placebos, the climate-A partialling robustness that breaks the GAEZ-implied correlation between climate and ancestral crop yield, cumulative conflict and volcanic-radiative-forcing controls, and the malaria-loci sensitivity.

The investigation places five pre-existing causal-identification literatures on a single panel and reveals a clean temporal-moment structure: climate matters at the Malthusian density margin via pathway selection; ancestral crop yield matters across the whole demographic-density trajectory; the Neolithic-ancestry fraction matters at the cumulative-density and demographic-transition margins; and the functional-allele bundle captures, in interpretable form, what the AG diversity measure was reaching for on the per-capita-income margin. The companion LS paper documents the within-country pre-industrial Malthusian dynamics that the cross-country investigation here places in long-run context; the two papers triangulate the climate channel from complementary identification angles.
